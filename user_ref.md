## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### How do I add my own music data?
* You can add your own music data by creating a .csv file and adding it to the data folder. Be sure to name the file **album-rankings.csv**. A tutorial on how to do this is located in the **Developer Guides** under **Walkthroughs For Developers** named [Creating a .csv File](/dev_task#creating-a-csv-file).


#### Is my listening information being sold?
* No, please rest assured that the application does not impede on privacy by selling your information.


#### The website says “Disconnected from the server.” what happened?
* The website likely timed out from inactivity, simply refresh the tab to restore connection.


#### It is too slow to scroll through all the drop-down options, is there a faster way to select options?
* Yes, you can use your cursor to click into the dropdown and delete the current option and type what you are looking for.


#### I can only see one slider on Number One Albums and cannot move them
* If you only see one slider on **Number One Albums**, it means that the left and right slider have overlapped. To fix this hold on the slider and move it to the left or right. When the sliders are overlapped it defaults to the last slider you touched as the one on top, which is restricted from moving past the opposing slider.

## Release Formats for Music
A glossary for music related definitions.

#### Album (Long Play/LP):

A collection of songs, which typically spans longer than 45mins. Music artists will typically hold this term for a major musical release.

#### Extended Play (EP):

A collection of songs, which typically spans 30mins or less. Usually much shorter than a full length album and a given artist might designate this term for a shorter but still important project.

#### Single:

Typically, a singular song or a set of 2-3 of them. An artist might give this term to a music release in order to preview or promote an upcoming project.

>Note: Single data is not currently supported by My Favorite Albums.

#### Live:

A recording of a song or group of songs that were played live at a venue or concert.

## Mediums for Storing Music
My Favorite Albums currently supports only information for vinyl status. However, you might find it useful to learn more about different methods that have been used to store music.

#### Vinyl:

A physical medium for music stored on a grooved plastic circular disc. This medium uses grooves on both sides of the disc to store analog sound information, which is played back via running a needle along its surface and spinning the disc.

Vinyl is typically referred to in terms of “LPs” which is a designation for how many discs there are for a given album/EP. For example a 2LP has two discs.

![vinyl.jpg](/images/vinyl.jpg)

#### Cassette:

A physical medium for music stored on a magnetic tape, housed in a flat rectangular plastic. The tape is wound between two spools and has an A and B side that can be chosen by flipping the
orientation of the cassette.

![cass.jpg](/images/cass.jpg)

#### Compact Disc (CD):

A physical medium for music stored on an etched disc. CDs are played back through a laser reading the etchings on the disc, interpreting these etchings as 1’s and 0’s. This medium is one of the most common platforms for lossless audio.

![cd.jpg](/images/cd.jpg)

#### MP3:

A digital medium for music that compresses audio data into a very small file. This makes distributing and storing music significantly easier.
